# Kestrel Air: holiday strike plan (memo to the board)

*Figures come from the hand-in runs: plan_1, plan_2, nodecision_1, nodecision_2 and plan_nosurprises. That is 25 simulated customers, two seeds per plan, and 95% bootstrap intervals.*

## 1. What we decided, and why
| When | Action (one per decision) | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 7** (Dec 14-20) | Free changes and cancellations for bookings in a 2-week window | $0.2M |
| **Week 8** (Dec 21-27), strike week | Backup ground handling contract at both bases for the strike days | $0.5M |
| | **Total** | **$0.7M of $3M** |

- **The week-7 message** names the strike days and says the union may extend the strike. It lets passengers move their flight for free and points them to days before Dec 22. It promises only what we already do: the next Kestrel seat, or a full refund.
- **The week-8 message** says backup crews will keep most flights running and that some may still be late.
- **Why this pair.** Passengers forgive a late flight but leave when they are stranded. Of four one-action pairs, free changes plus backup crews lost the fewest customers. That held in both seeds, under our planned strike and under a strike extended to Dec 24.
- **No week-5 decision.** Three grounded aircraft caused cancellations in Thanksgiving week, before any decision could act. Testing a leased aircraft, free changes or loyalty points in week 5 did not beat keeping the slot for the strike.

## 2. What the simulation says
Plan compared with doing nothing (business as usual). The range is the 95% bootstrap interval over both seeds.

| | Do nothing | This plan |
|---|---|---|
| Customers lost (moved to another airline or canceled the trip) | 24.5% [14-35] | **15.0% [6-25]** |
| Fare revenue kept, weeks 5-10 (simulated passengers) | 72.1 [62-84] | **85.4 [74-96]** (+13.4) |
| Blaming Kestrel, weeks 8-10 | 36% | 29% |
| Net-promoter proxy, change vs doing nothing | | +8.5 |

- **The direction holds in both seeds** for loss, revenue, blame and complaints. The intervals overlap, so the size is uncertain.
- **Strike-day passengers:**
  - We lose 60% and 6% of them under the plan (seed 1 / seed 2).
  - We lose 73% and 50% if we do nothing.
- **Hotels instead of backup crews:**
  - Hotels lost more strike-day passengers in both seeds (73% / 22% vs 60% / 6%).
  - Under the plan's revenue kept was 72 / 98; with hotels it was 69 / 89.
- **If the strike runs to Dec 24:** backup crews still lose the fewest strike-day passengers (50% / 18%, vs 54% / 29% with hotels and 77% / 36% doing nothing).
- **Flights operated (goal 1, target 98%): about 98.3% for the fleet.** Three of 36 aircraft out for two of the ten weeks is about 1.7% of flights.
  - The simulation's own figure (about 90%) counts only the ~33 flights our simulated customers take, so 3-4 engine cancellations weigh 10%.
  - The grounding happened in weeks 4-5. No later decision can change it.
  - If the strike is extended to Dec 24, that day is not excluded from the goal. Backup crews are what keep flights running on that day (see the extended-strike result above).
- **The engine grounding has a real cost.** In the same runs without the grounding, flights operated fall from about 99% to 92-94%, and customers lost rise by 5-14 points.

## 3. What the simulation cannot tell you
- **Flight outcomes are our assumptions.** How many flights operate, and how much backup crews cut cancellations (to 35% of the strike-day rate), are assumptions, not results.
- **The sample is small.** 25 simulated customers stand in for 210,000 passengers a week, weighted by our own estimate of the customer mix. One customer moves revenue by about 5 points.
- **Simulated customers are not real passengers.** Run twice on identical inputs they agree on 97.6% of weekly choices; that is consistency, not accuracy.
- **The simulation ends on January 10.** We cannot see behaviour after that.

## 4. What to watch next
- **Weeks 7-8:**
  - whether the union extends the strike;
  - backup crew numbers confirmed at both airports by Dec 20;
  - hours from cancellation to new seat;
  - call-center wait.
- **Thanksgiving passengers whose flights were canceled.** They are the largest unhappy group, and February bookings will show whether they come back.
- **If the strike is extended:** about $2.3M of the budget is unspent, enough for hotel rooms ($0.2M) or a partner rebooking deal ($0.25M) in week 9.
