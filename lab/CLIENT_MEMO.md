# Kestrel Air: holiday strike plan (memo to the board)

*Draft. Figures are from the comparison runs (25 simulated customers, two seeds each); they will be updated with the final runs.*

## 1. What we decided, and why
| When | Actions | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 7** (Dec 14-20), before the strike | Backup ground handling at both bases for the strike days · extra airport and call-center staff · free changes and cancellations for travel Dec 14-27 | $0.85M |
| **Week 8** (Dec 21-27), strike week | Rebooking deal with a partner airline · hotel rooms held for anyone stranded, whatever the cause | $0.45M |
| | **Total** | **$1.3M of $3M** |

Every message says only what we know and what these actions deliver, and states refund rights plainly. **Why this plan:** in our simulation, passengers forgive a late flight; they leave when they are stranded without a seat, a bed or an answer. The plan attacks those three: fewer disrupted flights, faster rebooking, no night on the airport floor.

## 2. What the simulation says
Compared with doing nothing (business as usual), with 95% intervals:

| | Do nothing | This plan |
|---|---|---|
| Customers lost (moved to another airline or canceled the trip) | 23.5% [9-40] | **5.0% [0-11]** |
| Blaming Kestrel, weeks 8-10 | 36% | 11% |
| Fare revenue kept, weeks 5-10 (simulated passengers) | 68.7 [46-91] | 90.9 [78-100] (+22 points) |

- **Customers lost is the strongest result:** the two intervals barely touch. Revenue and the net-promoter proxy move the same way, but their intervals overlap: the direction holds across both seeds, the size does not.
- **Hotel rooms alone change behaviour.** With the same flights disrupted, stranded customers who got a room stayed. Direction consistent over two seeds; size uncertain (20 customers, overlapping intervals).
- **Warning early without an operational fix backfires.** Free cancellation announced in week 6, on its own, led more customers to cancel. Direction consistent over two seeds; size uncertain.
- **Engine inspection (week 3 news):** under a mild and a severe reading, customers did not change what they did; blame rose slightly. We did not spend a decision on it.

## 3. What the simulation cannot tell you
- How many flights operate and how fast passengers get a new seat are **our assumptions**, not simulation results. The backup crews' effect on cancellations is the biggest one.
- 25 simulated customers stand in for 210,000 passengers a week, weighted by our own estimate of the customer mix (no public split exists). One customer can move revenue by about 5 points.
- Simulated customers are not real passengers. Run twice on identical inputs, they agree on 97.6% of weekly choices, but that is consistency, not accuracy.
- We did **not** test a strike longer than 48 hours, or behaviour after January 10. The size of any loss is uncertain; the direction is what we trust.

## 4. What to watch next
- **Weeks 7-8:** hours from cancellation to new seat (the driver of loss in every run); call-center wait; DOT complaints per day; share of social posts that blame Kestrel rather than the contractor.
- **If the union extends the strike before week 8 starts:** the week-8 decision can still be changed; use part of the remaining $1.7M on a leased aircraft or travel credits for affected passengers.
- **After the strike:** February bookings by customers whose December flight was disrupted. The simulation ends on January 10; this is where real loss will show.
